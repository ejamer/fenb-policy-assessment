# Strategic Plan 2024–2028 — FENB

*Developed through member surveys, situational analysis, review of similar organizations' strategic plans, and input from provincial fencing leaders and the Board.*

---

## Mission

> *"To encourage people in New Brunswick to participate in and enjoy the sport of fencing, both recreationally and competitively, in a safe environment at a reasonable cost."*

## Vision Values

Community · Participation · Honour (fair play, sportsmanship) · Excellence · Experience (fun, safety) · Confidence / Character / Self-esteem

## End Statements
(Align exactly with GE-1 through GE-6 in the [[Board Policy Manual (Feb 2024)]])

- Fencers with knowledge, skills, attitudes to participate recreationally and/or compete provincially, nationally, internationally
- Opportunities for quality, financially accessible, recreational fencing
- Qualified coaches and officials throughout the fencing experience
- A fencing environment meeting quality, safety, technical, accountability standards
- A sustainable and fair not-for-profit community recognizing volunteer contributions
- An awareness and positive perspective of the sport of fencing

---

## Organizational Context

### Background
- Founded 1969 as NB Fencing Association
- Incorporated as FENB (NFP) in 2006
- Governance changed from Management Board to Carver Policy Board; ED appointed
- Bylaws revised to reflect changes

### Organizational Design
- 7 elected Board directors (President + 6)
- Employed Executive Director
- Athlete Development Committee (ADC) — appointed by Board; chair must be a Board member
- Coach Developer — appointed by Board; reports to Board via ED

## Five Strategic Focus Areas

### Strategy 1 — Support Clubs to Grow Membership and Diversity
**Goal:** Increase membership 10% annually; grow clubs from 6 to 8 by 2027-28; +30% gender diversity by 2027-28

**Key Actions:**
- School and community fencing outreach programs in partnership with local clubs
- Targeted training/competition/social opportunities for girls and adults
- Promotional material emphasizing diversity
- New club pathway program (equipment, insurance, coaching development)
- Track member retention and satisfaction rates

**Targets:**
- 10% annual membership growth
- Satisfaction survey tracking by 2025-26
- 6 → 8 active clubs by 2027-28
- +30% gender diversity by 2027-28
- +2 new outreach programs/year; 50% re-offered the following year

### Strategy 2 — Increase Public Awareness and Communications
**Goal:** Increase public awareness via social media; improve internal/external communications

**Key Actions:**
- Publish all events/results on social media
- Update and modernize FENB website (**target: end of 2025** — now past)
- Social media public awareness campaign with clubs
- Post-event surveys for all tournaments and camps

**Targets:**
- Website modernization by end of 2025
- Provincial schedule finalized by end of August each season
- Schedule circulated 6 weeks in advance
- Post-event surveys for all FENB events
- Social media subcommittee established

### Strategy 3 — Value Safety and Respect
**Goal:** Zero-tolerance Safe Sport environment; 100% member and club adherence

**Key Actions:**
- Adopt/update Safe Sport policy yearly
- Mandate all members and clubs to sign Safe Sport agreement
- Enforce Safe Sport policies; provide complaint/remediation process

**Targets:**
- 100% members sign Safe Sport agreement
- 100% clubs adopt Safe Sport policy at club level
- All non-compliant incidents addressed in timely manner

### Strategy 4 — Develop Strong Leadership and Infrastructure
**Goal:** Stronger coaches, referees, armourers, and organizational succession

**Key Actions:**
- Coach development pathways with specific certification targets
- Referee development pathways
- Armourer and weapons training for each club
- Involve more people at executive level; succession planning for Board

**Targets:**
- Coach pathway facilitated by end of 2025 (CFF/CAC guidelines)
- Head coaches: competition-introduction certification by 2027-28
- Assistant coaches: instructor-beginner certification by 2027-28
- Comprehensive referee pathway launched by end of 2025 (with CFF)
- Armourer certification course within province by 2025-26
- Annual weapon care clinic at provincial events

### Strategy 5 — Support Athlete Development
**Goal:** Development programs for recreational, competitive, and HP athletes

**Key Actions:**
- Establish HP program for athletes aspiring to national/international competition
- Continue to support Provincial Team
- Maintain consistent provincial competition calendar
- Centralized tournament/camp registration

**Targets:**
- Recreational memberships grow at 10% overall membership target
- HP and provincial program athletes grow 10%/year
- Athletes at out-of-province competitions grow 10%/year
- 1-2 social events/year for recreational athletes

## Observations

### Website modernization target (end of 2025) has passed #gap
The strategic target to "complete modernization of the FENB website by end of 2025" was a specific, dated commitment. As of June 2026, the status of this target is unknown from the vault documents. The Hugo-based website under development (the source repo) may be the modernization effort.

### No policy framework for the ADC or HP Program #gap
Strategy 5 relies heavily on the Athlete Development Committee (ADC) and a new HP Program — but neither has a governing policy. The [[Bylaws (Nov 2024)]] makes the ADC optional ("if one exists"). There is no HP Program Policy in the current policy inventory. See [[Strategic Plan vs Policy Alignment]].

### The school program is the fastest-growing segment — but has no vote #gap
School members grew from 0 to 68 in one year (2023-24), representing 23% of total membership. These are "Special Recreational Fencing Members" under the [[Bylaws (Nov 2024)]] — they have no voting rights. If this segment continues to grow rapidly, it will represent an increasingly large share of participants who have no formal voice in the organization.

### Leadership succession is explicitly named as a strategic risk
The departure of Barbara Daniel (2021) and Martin Stoev's planned departure (early 2024) from Damocles — the largest Fredericton club — is cited explicitly as a situation-analysis concern. The volunteer dependency analysis (Section 2.4) also names the same individuals cycling through multiple roles. This is a real organizational fragility that no current policy directly addresses.

### Gender diversity gap vs. DEI targets
The membership data (72% male) reveals that achieving the DEI policy's goal of 40% gender representation at the Board/committee level while the membership pipeline is only 27% female will require active intervention. Strategy 1's "+30% gender diversity target by 2027-28" is ambitious in this context. See [[2.3 Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion]].
