# FENB Concussion Protocol

*Adapted from Parachute (2024), Canadian Guideline on Concussion in Sport, 2nd edition.*

> **Disclaimer:** Neither this document nor any linked documents constitute official medical advice, nor are they meant to replace any kind of medical diagnosis, symptom assessment, or medical opinion.

---

## Purpose
Guide the management of athletes who may have a suspected concussion as a result of participation in FENB activities. Covers recognition, medical diagnosis, and management. Aims to ensure timely and appropriate care and proper management to allow athletes to return to sport safely.

## Who Uses This Protocol
All individuals with a role interacting with athletes: athletes, parents/caregivers, coaches, officials, teachers, trainers, and licensed healthcare professionals.

---

## 1. Pre-Season Education

Annual education is required for all sport community members on:
- Definition of concussion
- Possible mechanisms of injury
- Common signs and symptoms
- Prevention steps
- What to do when a concussion is suspected
- Return-to-School and Return-to-Sport strategies
- Return-to-sport medical clearance requirements

**Implementation:** Pre-season in-person orientation sessions for athletes, parents/caregivers, coaches, and other sport stakeholders are the recommended delivery method.

---

## 2. Head Injury Recognition

**A concussion should be suspected if an athlete sustains an impact to the head, face, neck, or body AND:**
- Demonstrates one or more observable signs of suspected concussion (per CRT6), OR
- Reports one or more symptoms of suspected concussion (per CRT6)

This includes unwitnessed impacts where signs or symptoms are subsequently observed.

**Resources:**
- General information: https://parachute.ca/en/injury-topic/concussion/
- Concussion Recognition Tool 6 (CRT6): [CRT6 PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1YMiPeDpLZ7Kj-MzmadfE1hRKJeypFjte/view?usp=sharing)

---

## Key Return-to-Sport Provisions

*(Full protocol content is in the source file; the key framework is summarized here)*

The protocol follows the Parachute/Canadian Guideline framework which includes:
- Immediate removal from play upon suspected concussion
- No same-day return to sport
- Medical clearance required before return to contact/competition activities
- Graduated Return-to-School (RTS) and Return-to-Sport (RTP) strategies
- Medical professional oversight for formal diagnosis and clearance

---

## Integration with Policy 2.1

This protocol is referenced in [[2.1 Safe Sport, Conduct, and Athlete Protection]] (Section J), which requires:
- Coaches, officials, and relevant personnel must complete approved concussion-awareness training
- Athletes have the right to appropriate medical and mental-health support
- FENB will maintain access to referral resources and qualified professionals
- Medical emergencies or suspected trauma managed per [[2.17 Crisis and Emergency Management]]

## Observations

### This protocol has no formal approval date #gap
All 12 operational policies are dated "Approved April 2026." The Concussion Protocol has no approval date. It is adapted from a 2024 Parachute document and referenced in the April 2026 Safe Sport policy, suggesting it was current at that time — but without a formal approval date, the governance status of this document is ambiguous. It should be formally approved by the Board and dated.

### Concussion training requirement is in 2.1 but no tracking mechanism exists
Section J of [[2.1 Safe Sport, Conduct, and Athlete Protection]] requires coaches and officials to complete approved concussion-awareness training. Neither the HR policy (2.7) nor the Volunteer Management policy (2.10) specifies how this training is tracked, which platform is used, or what constitutes "approved" training. This is a compliance gap — the obligation exists but the mechanism to verify it doesn't.

### The protocol is separate from the sport injury emergency response
The [[2.17 Crisis and Emergency Management]] policy would govern a serious head injury during an event (emergency response, incident reporting, insurer notification). The Concussion Protocol governs recognition and return-to-sport management. These two instruments are complementary but not explicitly cross-referenced in both directions — 2.17 doesn't mention concussion management, and this protocol doesn't mention the incident reporting chain.
