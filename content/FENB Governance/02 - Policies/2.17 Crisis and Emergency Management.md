## Observations

### Two stale cross-references in this policy #inconsistency
This policy contains two references that no longer match the current policy numbering:

1. **"Safe Sport and Athlete Welfare Policy (Section 2.13)"** — Section 2.13 does not exist in the current policy manual. The Safe Sport policy is now [[2.1 Safe Sport, Conduct, and Athlete Protection]], which appears to be a consolidation of what was formerly 2.13 (Safe Sport and Athlete Welfare) and other policies. The reference was not updated when the consolidation occurred in April 2026.

2. **"Communications and Social Media Policy (Section 2.1)"** — Section 2.1 now refers to the consolidated Safe Sport policy, which includes communications standards in Section E. The standalone Communications and Social Media policy no longer exists as a separate section. Same stale reference as in [[2.11 Privacy and Data Protection]].

These two cross-reference errors suggest that the April 2026 policy consolidation was done without a systematic find-and-replace of all references in the rest of the manual. See [[Policy Cross-Reference Map]] for the full picture.

### The FENB Crisis Management Protocol is referenced extensively but absent #gap
This policy is essentially a two-page governance wrapper pointing to the actual Crisis Management Protocol — but that Protocol is not in this vault and may not exist as a formal written document. The Policy says the Protocol "will be maintained" (future-oriented), which may signal it was being developed at the time of the April 2026 policy approval. Until the Protocol exists, this policy has no operational teeth. See [[Referenced but Absent Documents]].

### This policy is comparatively thin
At roughly 300 words, 2.17 is the shortest substantive policy in the manual. Given that it governs responses to incidents with the highest potential impact on FENB's reputation and participant welfare, it relies heavily on the absent Protocol. The annual review trigger ("following a significant crisis") is appropriate but means the Protocol needs to be drafted before the next incident, not after.

## Summary

### Purpose
Ensure FENB is prepared to respond effectively and safely to emergencies, crises, or critical incidents that could impact the health, safety, or reputation of participants, staff, or the organization. Provides a **governance framework** for crisis management and directs members to follow the detailed procedures in the ***FENB Crisis Management Protocol*** (a separate operational document).

### Scope
All FENB representatives — Board members, employees, coaches, officials, volunteers, and athletes — during any FENB activity, event, or operation where an emergency or crisis may occur.

### Key Provisions

**Crisis Preparedness**
- FENB will maintain an up-to-date crisis management protocol outlining procedures for emergency response, communication, and recovery
- ED is responsible for ensuring that all staff, coaches, and event organizers are familiar with the ***FENB Crisis Management Protocol*** and understand their roles

**Reporting and Communication**
- In any emergency: **safety of participants is the first priority**; incidents must be reported immediately to the ED or designate
- ED, in coordination with the Board, oversees external communications for accuracy, timeliness, and consistency per Communications and Social Media Policy (Section 2.1) ← *stale reference — see Observations*
- Media inquiries and public statements during crises are managed centrally by authorized representatives only

**Post-Crisis Review and Recovery**
- Following a crisis or emergency, FENB will conduct a debriefing and assessment to identify lessons learned
- Review includes consultation with those involved; aligns with overall risk management framework
- Corrective actions documented and incorporated into updated versions of the ***FENB Crisis Management Protocol***

**Review**
Every 2 years or following a significant crisis or change to the FENB Crisis Management Protocol.
