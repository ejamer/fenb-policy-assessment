# 2.5 Official Language Policy

**Approved April 2026**

## Purpose
Ensure FENB operates in both official languages of New Brunswick, recognizing equal status of English and French in all communications, programs, and services. Respects linguistic rights per the provincial Official Languages Act.

## Scope
All written, verbal, and digital communications produced or issued by FENB, including public events, official documents, online content, and correspondence. Applies to all FENB representatives and extends to affiliated clubs and programs when acting under FENB authority or branding.

## Key Provisions

**Bilingual Service Delivery**
- Public-facing communications, documentation, and services in both official languages
- Simultaneous bilingual release of public materials; urgent releases may follow with second language as soon as possible
- Event signage, forms, and registration materials for provincial competitions in both official languages

**Active Offer of Service**
- FENB will make it known at first point of contact that services are available in both languages
- Bilingual signage at events and meetings; bilingual greetings at registration desks; bilingual voicemail/email instructions

**Member Communication Rights**
- Members, parents, and participants may communicate in either English or French
- FENB acknowledges inquiries in the language received and responds in the same language
- Adequate bilingual capacity or interpretation support at major events including tournaments and AGM

**Documents Required in Both Languages**
- Constitution and bylaws
- Board-approved policies
- AGM notices, agendas, and minutes
- Annual reports
- Event rules, registration forms, and selection criteria
- Discipline, complaints, and safety-related information
- Standard agreements and forms made available in either official language upon request

**Training and Education**
- Training programs offered in both official languages where reasonably possible; interpretation or translated materials used to support bilingual delivery

**Partnerships**
- Collaborate with Sport NB, CFF, and other organizations to promote bilingualism and share best practices

**Oversight and Complaints**
- ED responsible for ensuring compliance, coordinating translation, interpretation, and bilingual delivery
- Concerns/complaints submitted to ED; summary of language-related issues may be reported to Board

## Observations

### Section 2.4 reference — absent policy #gap
This is one of only two places in the entire policy manual where Section 2.4 is explicitly named: "Equipment and School Program Policy (Section 2.4)". This confirms that 2.4 exists or was intended to exist. Given that the school program is the fastest-growing membership segment, the absence of this policy from the current policy set is notable. See [[Policy Inventory and Gap Register]].

### Bilingual obligations apply to this vault
Under this policy, all Board-approved policies must be maintained in both official languages. The source repo has `.en.md` and `.fr.md` versions of all policies. This vault currently only holds English versions. If this vault is to be treated as an authoritative governance workspace rather than just an analysis tool, French-language equivalents of policy notes should eventually be created.

### "Where reasonably possible" for training delivery
The policy qualifies bilingual training delivery with "where reasonably possible" — recognizing the practical constraint that FENB may not always have bilingual facilitators available. This is a practical concession, but it means some NB fencers may receive Safe Sport, coach, or referee training only in English. For NB, where there is a significant francophone community (especially in the Moncton/South-East region where Escrime La Résistance operates), this warrants tracking.
