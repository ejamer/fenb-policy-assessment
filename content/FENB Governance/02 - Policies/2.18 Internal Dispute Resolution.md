# 2.18 Internal Dispute Resolution Policy

**Approved April 2026**

> This policy covers disputes **outside** UCCMS jurisdiction. All UCCMS/maltreatment matters go to NBSSCM — this policy explicitly does **not** handle those.

## Purpose
Provide a fair, transparent, and timely process for resolving internal disputes within FENB that fall outside the jurisdiction of the UCCMS.

## Scope
All FENB "Participants" — athletes, coaches, officials, volunteers, Board members, and staff.

**What this policy COVERS:**
- Disagreements regarding interpretation of FENB policies
- Operational disputes (scheduling, equipment use, team selections)
- Minor conduct infractions not rising to the level of "Maltreatment"
- Volunteer or staff performance disagreements

**What this policy DOES NOT cover:**
- **Maltreatment** — any allegations of abuse, harassment, or discrimination as defined by the UCCMS → **must be referred to NBSSCM**
- **Technical officiating** — referee decisions during bouts → covered by FIE/CFF technical rules

---

## The Two-Path Triage System

| Path | Nature of Concern | Reporting Body |
|------|------------------|----------------|
| **Path A** | UCCMS violations, abuse, grooming, severe harassment | **NBSSCM / Independent Third Party** |
| **Path B** | Policy disputes, minor conduct, operational issues | **FENB Internal Process (this policy)** |

Triage is performed by the ED (or designated Board member) upon receipt of any formal complaint.

---

## Resolution Procedures (Path B)

### Stage 1: Informal Resolution / Mediation
- Initiated within **7 days** of the dispute
- Parties attempt resolution through coached dialogue or mediation
- If a signed agreement is reached → matter is closed

### Stage 2: Formal Internal Review
If informal resolution fails, a formal written complaint is submitted to the ED.

1. **Panel appointment:** Board appoints an unbiased **3-person Internal Review Panel**
2. **Right to be heard:** Both Complainant and Respondent may submit written evidence and call witnesses
3. **Timeliness:** Written decision rendered within **14 days** of the hearing

### Stage 3: Sanctions and Outcomes
If panel finds a violation, possible outcomes include:
- Verbal or written reprimand
- Requirement for additional training (e.g., Respect in Sport)
- Temporary suspension of membership or volunteer privileges
- Removal from a specific role (e.g., Board position or coaching role)

---

## Principles of Fairness ("Natural Justice")
- **Impartiality:** No panel member with a personal or professional conflict of interest
- **Confidentiality:** Information shared only with those directly involved in the resolution
- **Transparency:** All parties receive a copy of the written decision and reasoning

---

## Right to Appeal
Appeals are only permitted where there is evidence of:
- Failure to follow the procedures outlined in this policy
- A conflict of interest that influenced the decision
- A decision that was grossly unreasonable or biased

Appeals governed by the FENB Board of Directors; may ultimately be referred to Sport NB if internal options are exhausted.

A separate Triage Guide (for FENB Leadership) is embedded in the annex:

**Step 1 — Jurisdictional Check (The 24-Hour Rule)**
Within 24 hours of receipt, the ED must determine:
1. Is it UCCMS? → Forward immediately to NBSSCM or Abuse-Free Sport portal; ED must **not** investigate
2. Is it Internal? → File stays with FENB under this policy
3. Is it Technical? → Referred to the Directoire Technique (DT) or FENB Technical Committee

**Step 2 — Conflict of Interest Check**
No panel member from the same club as Complainant or Respondent, or with a documented personal history with either.

**Step 3 — Notification**
Respondent notified (redacted where necessary) and given **7 days** for a written response.

## Observations

### The 24-hour triage rule is a strong governance mechanism
The explicit 24-hour jurisdictional check with mandatory UCCMS referral is sophisticated and well-designed. It prevents FENB from inadvertently handling a maltreatment case internally (which would be a Safe Sport violation) and protects both the complainant and the organization. This is probably the most operationally mature provision in the entire policy manual.

### No defined "Technical Committee" or DT composition
The Internal Triage Guide refers to the "Directoire Technique (DT) or the FENB Technical Committee" for technical/field-of-play disputes. There is no policy, bylaw provision, or other document in this vault that defines the composition or authority of these bodies. Their existence appears to be assumed.

### Timeline tightness: 7+14 = 21 days maximum for Path B resolution
Stage 1 (7 days) + Stage 2 (14 days) = 21 days from dispute to decision in the ideal case. This is faster than many sport dispute resolution processes, which is commendable. However, for a volunteer-run organization, the ability to convene a 3-person unbiased panel within this timeframe may be challenging in practice — particularly the requirement that no panel member be from the same club as either party.

### Panel composition constraint is more challenging than it appears
With only 6 active clubs and many individuals serving multiple roles, finding three unbiased panelists who have no club affiliation with either party and no personal history could be genuinely difficult for complaints involving major clubs (DAM with 49 members, RST with 66). A pre-approved roster of trained panelists — perhaps drawn from outside the province or from Sport NB — would make this policy more reliable.

### This policy is reviewed jointly with Policy 2.10
The policy states it will be reviewed every two years "in conjunction with the Volunteer Management Policy" to ensure alignment with UCCMS v. 6.0. This is an unusual but sensible co-review commitment — the two policies form the operational spine of how FENB handles misconduct.
