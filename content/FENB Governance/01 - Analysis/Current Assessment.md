## 2026-06-09 — Initial Vault Population

*Based on: Bylaws (Nov 2024), Board Policy Manual (Feb 2024), Strategic Plan 2024-2028, 12 operational policies (all April 2026), Concussion Protocol (undated). The archived Operations Manual was not read.*

### What's working well

The April 2026 policy batch is a genuine governance achievement for a small volunteer-run sport organization. The Safe Sport policy (2.1) is the standout — consolidating Safe Sport, Code of Conduct, Anti-Harassment, and Communications into a single integrated framework is exactly the right move, and the result is coherent and sophisticated. The two-path triage system in the Dispute Resolution policy (2.18) — with the explicit 24-hour jurisdictional check and hard UCCMS referral — is well-designed and better than what many larger organizations have. The financial controls (dual-signature, 10% reallocation threshold, 30-day expense reconciliation) are appropriate for the organization's size and risk profile.

The Carver governance model is also well-suited to FENB. It gives the ED operational flexibility while keeping the Board focused on strategy and oversight. The monitoring schedule embedded in the Board Policy Manual is a real accountability mechanism — if it's actually being followed.

### Where the April 2026 overhaul fell short

The consolidation into the new 2.1 wasn't fully propagated. Two policies (2.11 and 2.17) still reference "Communications and Social Media Policy (Section 2.1)" as if it were a standalone, and 2.17 references "Safe Sport and Athlete Welfare Policy (Section 2.13)" — a section that no longer exists. These are editorial errors that will cause confusion for anyone new to the documents. They're easy fixes but need to happen before the 2028 review cycle.

The entire policy batch reads as governance infrastructure, not sport development policy. That's not wrong — governance infrastructure is what needed building. But it means the Strategic Plan's Strategies 4 and 5 (leadership/infrastructure development, athlete development) are essentially funded by goodwill and strategic intent rather than policy commitment. There's no Athlete Development Policy, no HP Program Policy, no coaching or referee certification policy, and no club governance standards. The organization has made good promises in its strategic plan about coach pathways, a referee pathway, and an armourer program — all with specific 2025 target dates — but none of those targets have a policy framework behind them and several of those dates have now passed.

### The governance architecture tension

The Bylaws describe the ED as a record-keeper (records, books, seal). The Carver Board Policy Manual grants the ED full operational authority. The 12 operational policies assign the ED 20+ specific accountability items. These three layers are not contradictory but they're unreconciled — someone reading only the Bylaws would not understand the ED's actual role. For a small organization that relies heavily on institutional memory and volunteers, this kind of undocumented governance gap is manageable until there's a transition (new ED, Board dispute, funding audit). It should be cleaned up.

The Board Policy Manual (last meaningfully revised in 2017) is also noticeably out of alignment with the more developed 2026 operational policies. GP-A4's "reasonable efforts" for Board diversity is weaker than the 40% gender target in the DEI policy. EDB-A7's one-sentence prohibition on abuse is dwarfed by the comprehensive Safe Sport framework in 2.1. The Board Policy Manual is due for a substantive revision that brings the Carver framework language into alignment with what the operational policies actually say.

### The biggest single gap

Policy 2.4 (Equipment and School Program) is explicitly referenced in the Official Language policy but does not exist. The school program grew from zero to 68 members in a single year — 23% of total membership — and these participants have no voting rights, operate under a policy that doesn't exist, and are the fastest-growing segment of the organization. This is the most pressing gap in the current policy set.

Close second: the FENB Crisis Management Protocol doesn't exist (or isn't available). Policy 2.17 is a two-page wrapper pointing entirely to it. Until the Protocol is written, FENB's "crisis management framework" has no operational content.

### What I don't know yet

The archived Operations Manual (`operations-manual-en.pdf` and `operations-manual-fr.pdf` in the source repo) has not been read. It was filed as "archived" but it may contain: the predecessor to the Crisis Management Protocol, historical club standards, and procedures that were either superseded or that remain the de facto operational standard because nothing has replaced them. Reading it would likely close several of the "absent document" gaps identified in this vault.

The actual compliance state is also unknown from documents alone. Are the monitoring reports being produced per the Carver schedule? Are the annual conflict-of-interest declarations being filed? Is the risk register being maintained? Do the school program participants have insurance coverage? These questions can't be answered from the policy documents — they require looking at actual operations.
