# Governance Architecture — Three-Layer Model

FENB operates a three-layer governance structure. Understanding which layer governs which decisions is essential for interpreting any individual policy.

---

## Layer 1 — Bylaws (Legal Foundation)

**Document:** [[Bylaws (Nov 2024)]]  
**Authority:** NB Companies Act; amended by 2/3 member vote with 21-day notice  
**What it governs:** Legal existence, purpose, membership structure and rights, Board composition and elections, AGM and Special GM rules, signing authority, fiscal year, dissolution

**Key characteristics:**
- The *constitution* — all other documents derive authority from it
- Cannot be overridden by Board resolution alone
- Amendments require member participation and supermajority
- Last amended November 2024

**Tension point:** The Bylaws describe the ED's duties very narrowly (records, books, seal, co-signature). This was written before or without reflecting the Carver governance model adopted in the Board Policy Manual, which gives the ED broad operational authority. See [[ED Authority and Accountability]].

---

## Layer 2 — Board Policy Manual (Carver Governance Instrument)

**Document:** [[Board Policy Manual (Feb 2024)]]  
**Authority:** Board of Directors resolution; can be amended by Board without member vote  
**What it governs:** How the Board governs (GP); what outcomes FENB exists to produce (GE/Ends); what the ED may not do (EDB/Executive Limitations); the Board-ED relationship (BEDR)

**Key characteristics:**
- Uses the **Carver Policy Governance** model — the Board sets Ends and Boundaries; the ED has full operational authority within those constraints
- Higher-level and more abstract than the operational policies
- The monitoring schedule (p. 12) is embedded here and governs ED accountability cadence
- Last revised February 2024 (only GP-D1 changed); revision history goes back to 2007

**What this layer does NOT do:**
- Does not prescribe specific operational procedures
- Does not assign specific accountabilities for each policy domain
- Does not define what "Safe Sport" means operationally

---

## Layer 3 — Operational Policies (Section 2.x)

**Documents:** 12 policies, all Approved April 2026  
**Authority:** Board approval; reviewed every 2 years  
**What they govern:** Specific operational domains — Safe Sport, risk, DEI, language, financial management, HR, volunteers, privacy, insurance, ethics, crisis management, dispute resolution

**Key characteristics:**
- More prescriptive than the Board Policy Manual; assign specific responsibilities to named roles (ED, Treasurer, President)
- All approved in a single batch (April 2026) — suggests a comprehensive policy overhaul rather than incremental updates
- Several policies explicitly consolidate former standalone policies (most notably 2.1)
- Cross-reference each other extensively — Safe Sport 2.1 is the hub

---

## Layer 4 — Protocols and Operational Documents

**Documents:** FENB Crisis Management Protocol (absent), Concussion Protocol, FENB Pay Scale (Google Docs), Complaint Intake Form  
**Authority:** ED operational authority per BEDR-C1  
**What they govern:** Specific procedural detail within policy areas

---

## How the Layers Interact

```
Bylaws (Layer 1)
  └─ Board Policy Manual (Layer 2)
       ├─ Global Ends → what success looks like
       ├─ ED Boundaries → what ED must not do
       └─ BEDR → how Board holds ED accountable
            └─ Operational Policies (Layer 3)
                 ├─ Define specific rules within Boundaries
                 └─ Assign specific accountabilities
                      └─ Protocols and Documents (Layer 4)
                           └─ Specific procedures
```

A conflict between layers is resolved by the higher layer. In practice:
- If a Board Policy Manual provision (e.g., EDB-B1 financial safety net) and an Operational Policy (e.g., 2.6 Financial Management) address the same issue differently, the Board Policy Manual prevails
- If the Bylaws and the Board Policy Manual conflict, the Bylaws prevail
- The ED can create further Layer 4 documents as long as they are consistent with all higher layers (BEDR-C1)

## Key Structural Issues

### 1. The Carver monitoring schedule is disconnected from Layer 3
The monitoring schedule in the [[Board Policy Manual (Feb 2024)]] (p. 12 table) establishes the cadence at which the ED must report to the Board on each EDB policy area. None of the Layer 3 operational policies reference this schedule. This means:
- A Board member who only reads the operational policies doesn't know that semi-annual monitoring of member treatment, quarterly monitoring of financial condition, and direct inspection of ED-Board communications are Board-mandated
- The monitoring schedule may not be consistently followed if it isn't part of the regular operational policy review cycle

**Recommendation:** Add a cross-reference to the monitoring schedule in [[2.6 Financial Management]] and [[2.2 Risk Management]] at minimum, or create a standalone "Board Monitoring Calendar" note in this vault.

### 2. The April 2026 batch approval of all operational policies suggests a one-time overhaul
All 12 policies share the same approval date. This is unusual — policies are normally reviewed and updated on a rolling basis. The simultaneous approval suggests a deliberate governance strengthening exercise (possibly prompted by Safe Sport requirements or Sport NB compliance). The consolidation of 2.1 into a single integrated Safe Sport policy was particularly significant.

The risk: policies developed as a batch may have internal inconsistencies (the stale cross-references to former policy numbers are evidence of this). A systematic quality review of all cross-references should be completed before the April 2028 review cycle.

### 3. Layer 4 is the weakest layer
Several Layer 4 documents are either absent (Crisis Management Protocol), externally hosted (Pay Scale in Google Docs), or undated (Concussion Protocol). Layer 4 documents are critical for operational effectiveness — they're what people actually use in the moment. See [[Referenced but Absent Documents]].

### 4. The Board Policy Manual is due for alignment with Layer 3
The Board Policy Manual was last substantively revised in 2017 (with a minor GP-D1 update in 2024). The Layer 3 policies are much more developed than what the Board Policy Manual contemplated. Specifically:
- GP-A4 (Board Inclusivity) is weaker than [[2.3 Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion]] (40% gender target)
- EDB-A7 (freedom from abuse) is one sentence; [[2.1 Safe Sport, Conduct, and Athlete Protection]] is now a comprehensive multi-section framework
- The monitoring schedule (BEDR-D3) uses different terminology and structure than the current operational policies

A full alignment review between Layers 2 and 3 is recommended at the next Board Policy Manual revision.
