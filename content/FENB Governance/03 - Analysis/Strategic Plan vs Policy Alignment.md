# Strategic Plan vs Policy Alignment

This note maps each of the five strategic focus areas in the [[Strategic Plan 2024-2028]] against the existing policy framework to identify where strategy has policy support and where it doesn't.

---

## Strategy 1 — Support Clubs to Grow Membership and Diversity

**Goal:** Increase membership 10% annually; grow clubs 6 → 8 by 2027-28; +30% gender diversity by 2027-28

| Strategic Action/Target | Policy Support | Gap |
|------------------------|----------------|-----|
| School and community outreach programs | Partial — [[2.5 Official Language]] references the absent Equipment and School Program Policy (2.4) | **Policy 2.4 is absent** |
| New club pathway program (equipment, insurance, coaching) | [[2.12 Insurance and Legal Compliance]] covers insurance requirements | No Club Affiliation Standards policy |
| +30% gender diversity target by 2027-28 | [[2.3 Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion]] supports this; 40% Board target | No accountability mechanism; DEI policy requires annual reporting on progress |
| Track member retention and satisfaction | None | No membership management or data policy; tracking mechanism unclear |
| Inclusive recruitment for underrepresented groups | [[2.3 Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion]], [[2.10 Volunteer Management]] | Coverage adequate |

**Verdict:** Strategy 1 is partially covered. The biggest gap is the absent Policy 2.4 governing school programs (the fastest-growing segment) and the lack of any club governance standards policy.

---

## Strategy 2 — Increase Public Awareness and Communications

**Goal:** Website modernization (by end of 2025); social media strategy; post-event surveys

| Strategic Action/Target | Policy Support | Gap |
|------------------------|----------------|-----|
| Social media presence and public awareness campaigns | [[2.1 Safe Sport, Conduct, and Athlete Protection]] §E (Communications and Social Media) | Governance framework exists but no standalone communications strategy |
| FENB website modernization by end of 2025 | [[2.1 Safe Sport]] §E: website reviewed at least annually; current information required | **Target date has passed** — status unknown |
| Post-event surveys | None | No policy framework for member feedback mechanisms |
| Provincial schedule finalized by August each season | None | No scheduling policy |
| Social media subcommittee | None | Subcommittee creation is informal; no policy mandate |

**Verdict:** Strategy 2 has minimal policy support. The communications obligations in [[2.1 Safe Sport]] §E are governance guardrails (what not to do, what must be kept current), not a strategy for building awareness. A standalone communications policy — possibly filling the gap at 2.8 or 2.9 — would be appropriate.

**Specific concern:** The website modernization target (end of 2025) was a specific, dated commitment in the strategic plan. As of June 2026, this target's status is unknown. The Hugo site project (the source repo) may be this modernization, but there is no governance record confirming completion or a revised timeline.

## Strategy 3 — Value Safety and Respect

**Goal:** 100% member Safe Sport agreement; 100% club adoption; zero-tolerance environment

| Strategic Action/Target | Policy Support | Gap |
|------------------------|----------------|-----|
| 100% members sign Safe Sport agreement | [[2.1 Safe Sport, Conduct, and Athlete Protection]] — comprehensive | Agreement/signature tracking mechanism not described |
| 100% clubs adopt Safe Sport policy | [[2.1 Safe Sport]] applies to clubs when acting under FENB authority/branding | No club-level Safe Sport adoption verification mechanism |
| Complaint/remediation process | [[2.18 Internal Dispute Resolution]], NBSSCM referral | Well-covered; triage system is solid |
| Annual Safe Sport policy update | [[2.1 Safe Sport]] §K: reviewed every 2 years (not yearly as strategy implies) | Minor discrepancy — policy says 2 years; strategy says yearly |
| Concussion management | [[Concussion Protocol]], [[2.1 Safe Sport]] §J | Protocol undated; training tracking mechanism absent |

**Verdict:** Strategy 3 has the strongest policy support of the five strategies. The policy framework is comprehensive. The main implementation gap is verification/tracking — how FENB confirms that members have signed, clubs have adopted, and training has been completed.

**Note:** The strategic plan says "adopt and update Safe Sport policy on a **yearly** basis." Policy 2.1 says it will be reviewed every **two years**. This is a minor inconsistency but suggests the strategic plan's ambition (annual refresh) is more demanding than the policy's review cycle.

## Strategy 4 — Develop Strong Leadership and Infrastructure

**Goal:** Coach certification pathways; referee pathways; armourer program; Board succession

| Strategic Action/Target | Policy Support | Gap |
|------------------------|----------------|-----|
| Coach development pathways | [[2.7 Human Resources]] §2 (screening/training), [[2.10 Volunteer Management]] §3 (orientation) | No coach development or certification policy; strategy targets specific CFF/CAC certification levels |
| Referee development pathways | None | No referee policy at all |
| Armourer and weapons training | None | No armourer policy or program standards |
| Succession planning for Board | None | [[2.7 Human Resources]] explicitly omits succession planning; [[2.10 Volunteer Management]] mentions development but not succession |
| Coach Developer role (reports to Board via ED) | [[Strategic Plan 2024-2028]] §1.4 describes this role | No policy defining the Coach Developer's mandate, authority, or accountability |

**Verdict:** Strategy 4 has the weakest policy support. The entire infrastructure development agenda — coaching certification, referee pathways, armourer training, succession planning — has no policy home. These are operational activities that the ED could implement under BEDR-C1 authority, but without policy-level commitment they remain discretionary rather than mandated.

**Specific dated targets missed or at risk:**
- Coach pathway facilitated by end of 2025 — status unknown
- Referee pathway by end of 2025 (with CFF) — status unknown
- Armourer course by 2025-26 — status unknown (current date: June 2026)

## Strategy 5 — Support Athlete Development

**Goal:** HP program; Provincial Team support; 10% growth in HP/provincial athletes; recreational athlete community

| Strategic Action/Target | Policy Support | Gap |
|------------------------|----------------|-----|
| Establish HP program | None | **No HP Program Policy**; Athlete Development Representative is in the Bylaws but no ADC policy exists |
| Continue Provincial Team support | None | No Provincial Team policy; no selection criteria policy |
| Centralized tournament/camp registration | None | No competition/event administration policy |
| 10% annual growth in HP/provincial athletes | [[2.3 Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion]] (general access principles) | No athlete development tracking framework |
| 1-2 social events/year for recreational athletes | None | Appropriate for operational discretion; no policy needed |

**Verdict:** Strategy 5 has essentially no policy support. This is the strategy with the largest policy gap. The Athlete Development Representative is formally named in the [[Bylaws (Nov 2024)]] (§18), giving athlete development a structural voice at the Board level — but without a corresponding policy framework, the ADC's mandate, the HP program's standards, and team selection criteria all exist only as strategic aspirations.

---

## Summary Assessment

| Strategy | Policy Coverage | Primary Gap |
|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| 1. Clubs/Membership | Moderate | Policy 2.4 (School Programs) absent; no Club Affiliation Standards |
| 2. Communications | Weak | No standalone Communications Policy; website target past |
| 3. Safety/Respect | Strong | Tracking/verification mechanisms absent |
| 4. Leadership/Infrastructure | Very Weak | No coaching, referee, armourer, or succession policies |
| 5. Athlete Development | Very Weak | No Athlete Development, HP Program, or Selection Policy |

The pattern is clear: the April 2026 policy overhaul prioritized governance infrastructure (Safe Sport, risk, HR, financial management, ethics) and was less focused on the sport development and program policies that Strategies 4 and 5 require. These represent the next logical phase of policy development.
