## Observations

### Annual volunteer recognition plan — does it exist? #gap
Section 8 commits FENB to implementing "an annual volunteer recognition plan aligned with Sport NB practices." There is no evidence in the vault of such a plan existing. Given that FENB is heavily volunteer-dependent (see [[Strategic Plan 2024-2028]] §2.4) and that volunteer burnout/overreliance on the same individuals is explicitly named as an organizational risk, this plan matters.

### Policy 2.7 and 2.10 overlap on screening
Both HR (2.7) and Volunteer Management (2.10) specify the same 3-year vulnerable sector check renewal requirement and Safe Sport training prerequisite. There is no friction between these policies but the duplication means updates need to be made in two places. A single screening standard (possibly housed in 2.1) would reduce this maintenance burden.

### FENB's volunteer reliance is an existential risk not fully addressed
The [[Strategic Plan 2024-2028]] names the problem explicitly: the same individuals frequently fill multiple roles; some major club-level coaching transitions have created instability. This policy addresses volunteer management well but doesn't include a succession or capacity-planning dimension that would address the strategic risk.

## Summary

### Purpose
Establish clear and consistent standards for the recruitment, screening, orientation, support, and recognition of volunteers. Ensures volunteer involvement is safe, equitable, and aligned with FENB's mission and Safe Sport obligations. Aligns with NBSSCM, UCCMS, and Sport NB principles of impartiality, fairness, respect, timeliness, and transparency.

### Scope
All individuals who volunteer with FENB: Board members, committee members, event officials, coaches, managers, and program assistants.

### Key Provisions

**1. Volunteer Recruitment and Selection**
- Equitable, barrier-free, inclusive recruitment consistent with provincial recreation and sport standards
- All volunteer opportunities openly communicated and clearly defined
- Role descriptions include responsibilities, qualifications, expected behaviours, and time commitments
- Active consideration of individuals from underrepresented groups, per DEI commitments
- Selection based on suitability, skills, values alignment, and positive community contribution

**2. Screening and Background Checks**
- Screening varies by level of trust, authority, and contact with participants
- Screening tools include: interviews, reference checks, police criminal record checks, **vulnerable sector checks (renewed every 3 years)**, confirmation of Safe Sport training
- ED or designated officer maintains all screening documentation confidentially
- Individuals not meeting screening requirements may not volunteer in roles involving participants, finances, or authority

**3. Orientation and Training**
- Orientation covers FENB policies, Safe Sport expectations, communication standards, privacy requirements, Rule of Two guidelines
- Volunteers must complete **recognized Safe Sport training** (e.g., CAC Safe Sport module)
- New volunteers receive mentorship and role-specific guidance from supervisors/senior volunteers

**4. Conduct, Behaviour, and Accountability**
- Must follow Code of Conduct, Safe Sport and Athlete Welfare Policy, Anti-Harassment Policy, and DEI Policy
- Must adhere to Safe Sport behavioural expectations:
  - Rule of Two compliance
  - Appropriate physical interactions
  - Respectful language, boundaries, and communication practices
  - Parent-inclusive or group messaging for communication with minors
- Violations addressed through disciplinary procedures including suspension or removal

**5. Reporting, Complaints, and Dispute Resolution — Two-Path System**
- **Path A (UCCMS):** Safe Sport and maltreatment complaints → NBSSCM or designated independent bodies; volunteers informed of access mechanism and rights
- **Path B (Internal):** Non-UCCMS concerns → FENB Internal Dispute Resolution Process per [[2.18 Internal Dispute Resolution]]

**6. Volunteer Supervision and Support**
- Supervisors ensure volunteers understand duties and have tools needed
- Supervisors monitor Safe Sport compliance including Rule of Two and communication boundaries
- Volunteers may request additional support, training, or clarification at any time

**7. Volunteer Development**
- Access to officiating clinics, coaching courses, leadership training, and mentorship programs
- FENB may financially support development where appropriate and resources permit

**8. Volunteer Recognition**
- Recognition may include public acknowledgments, certificates, annual awards
- FENB will implement an **annual volunteer recognition plan** aligned with Sport NB practices

**9. Privacy and Confidentiality**
- Screening results, personal data, and disciplinary matters kept strictly confidential
- Volunteers granted access only to personal information necessary for their duties
