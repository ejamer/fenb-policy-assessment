## Observations

### This policy is the cross-reference hub — and some references pointing to it are stale #inconsistency
Nearly every other policy refers back to 2.1. However, 2.1 absorbed what was formerly a standalone Communications and Social Media policy. Policies [[2.11 Privacy and Data Protection]] and [[2.17 Crisis and Emergency Management]] still reference "Communications and Social Media Policy (Section 2.1)" as if it were a standalone — which it now is, technically correct, but the label used in those cross-references implies a separate policy that no longer exists. This should be clarified in the next policy review cycle.

### Section 2.13 conflict
Policy [[2.17 Crisis and Emergency Management]] references "Safe Sport and Athlete Welfare Policy (Section 2.13)" — a section number that does not exist in the current policy manual. This strongly suggests that 2.13 was the former standalone Safe Sport policy and 2.1 is the result of consolidating it (along with other policies) in April 2026. The cross-reference in 2.17 was not updated. See [[Policy Cross-Reference Map]].

### Annual website content review is an ED obligation
Section E requires website content be reviewed "at least annually." This is an operational obligation that should be included in the ED's annual compliance checklist, cross-referenced with the Carver monitoring schedule in [[Board Policy Manual (Feb 2024)]].

### The duty to report failure is disciplinable
Section C explicitly makes failure to report a known or reasonably suspected Safe Sport violation a disciplinable offence. This is an important and sophisticated provision — it closes the "I didn't want to get involved" gap — but it requires that all members actually know about this duty. The pre-season education and orientation requirements in Section I are therefore critical to making this enforceable.

## Summary

### Purpose

Ensures all FENB activities are conducted in a safe, respectful, inclusive, and ethical environment, free from abuse, harassment, maltreatment, discrimination, intimidation, and retaliation.

This is a **consolidated policy** — it integrates what were previously separate policies on:
- Safe Sport and athlete welfare
- Code of conduct and ethical behaviour
- Anti-harassment and anti-discrimination
- Communications and social media conduct

Aligns with: **UCCMS** (Universal Code of Conduct to Prevent and Address Maltreatment in Sport), **CSSP** (Canadian Safe Sport Program), and the **CFF Safe Sport Policy Manual**.

---

### Scope

Applies to all individuals in FENB activities:
- Athletes, coaches, athlete support personnel, officials/referees
- Volunteers, Board/committee members, employees and contractors
- Parents, guardians, and spectators
- Affiliated clubs when acting under FENB authority or branding

Applies to conduct occurring:
- During training, competitions, events, programs, clinics, travel, meetings
- In physical, digital, and virtual environments (including social media)
- At provincial and club level where FENB governance or sanctioning applies
- Where conduct may reasonably impact participant safety, dignity, welfare, or FENB's reputation

---

### Key Provisions

#### A — Safe Sport Principles
- All participants have the right to participate free from abuse, neglect, harassment, discrimination, and retaliation
- Welfare, safety, and long-term development of athletes (especially minors) take priority over competitive/organizational objectives
- Power imbalances must be recognized and never abused
- Prevention, education, reporting, and enforcement are shared responsibilities
- Standards apply equally in physical, digital, and virtual environments

#### B — Standards of Conduct
All individuals must:
- Conduct themselves with integrity, honesty, respect, and fairness
- Respect the dignity and rights of all participants regardless of race, gender, ability, age, sexual orientation, or belief
- Avoid behaviour that could damage FENB's reputation or violate laws, contracts, or organizational standards
- Use FENB resources, authority, property, equipment, records, and systems responsibly
- Report any conduct known or suspected to be contrary to this policy

**Individuals in positions of authority (coaches, officials, Board members, staff, team leaders) have heightened responsibilities.**

#### C — Prohibited Conduct
- Physical, psychological, or emotional maltreatment
- Sexual harassment, exploitation, or assault
- Neglect, humiliation, or inappropriate discipline
- Grooming behaviours or boundary violations
- Bullying, hazing, coercion, or abuse of authority
- Discrimination on the basis of protected grounds
- Retaliation or intimidation against reporters or participants
- Interference with reporting or investigation processes
- Knowingly making false or malicious complaints
- **Failure to report known or reasonably suspected Safe Sport violations when a duty to report exists** (failure to report may itself result in disciplinary action)

#### D — Rule of Two
- At least two screened adults present during interactions with athletes
- One-on-one interactions held in observable and interruptible environments
- Applies to training, competitions, travel, locker rooms, and virtual settings
- Screening/renewal per [[2.7 Human Resources]] and [[2.10 Volunteer Management]]

#### E — Communications and Social Media
**Governance:**
- FENB must appoint a Webmaster/Communications Officer; if vacant, ED assumes responsibility
- Board must approve all official public statements, media releases, or formal responses

**Operational standards:**
- Website must contain current information on programs, competitions, events, policies, committees, contacts
- Website content reviewed **at least annually**
- Comply with CASL (Canadian Anti-Spam Legislation)

**Safe Sport controls:**
- No one-on-one electronic communication with athletes under 18 unless Rule of Two conditions met
- Communication with minors normally through parents/guardians
- Photos/videos of minors only with proper consent and without identifying information
- Association social media accounts monitored regularly; inappropriate content addressed promptly

#### F — Reporting Obligations
Reports may be made to:
- The Executive Director
- NB Safe Sport Complaint Mechanism (NBSSCM): https://www.sportnb.com/nb-safe-sport-complaint-mechanism/
- Canadian Safe Sport Program / OSIC (for UCCMS jurisdiction): https://cces.ca/report-safe-sport

Anonymous and third-party reports will be considered when sufficient information is available.

#### G — Investigation, Discipline, and Authority
- All reports treated seriously and handled confidentially
- Managed with procedural fairness for all parties
- Investigated internally or by independent third party as appropriate
- Records managed per [[2.11 Privacy and Data Protection]]
- Material safety/legal/reputational risks escalated per [[2.17 Crisis and Emergency Management]]

Disciplinary outcomes may include: removal from activities/events, suspension or termination of membership, permanent ineligibility, referral to external authorities.

#### H — Protection from Retaliation
Retaliation against anyone making a good-faith report or participating in an investigation is strictly prohibited and constitutes a serious breach.

#### I — Education, Training, and Prevention
- Ongoing education on Safe Sport, ethical conduct, reporting obligations, appropriate boundaries, communications
- Training tracked and documented per [[2.7 Human Resources]] and [[2.10 Volunteer Management]]

#### J — Concussion, Medical, and Mental-Health Support
- FENB enforces its [[Concussion Protocol]]
- Coaches, officials, and relevant personnel must complete approved concussion-awareness training
- Athletes have the right to appropriate medical and mental-health support and access to referral resources
- Medical emergencies or suspected trauma managed per [[2.17 Crisis and Emergency Management]]
